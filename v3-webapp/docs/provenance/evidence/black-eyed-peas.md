# Black-Eyed Peas / Cowpeas: Evidence Review

**Status:** Evidence recorded; no ingredient value, compatibility flag, or safety rule changed.

## Existing project record

The preserved ingredient record describes `black_eyed_peas` as “Safe dry, high protein.” The preserved toxic-legume note does not list black-eyed peas among its safe or unsafe raw-legume lists. This leaves the original project documentation internally incomplete for this ingredient.

## External evidence

University of Kentucky poultry guidance identifies cowpeas as *Vigna unguiculata*, also called black-eyed peas, and states that they are suitable for use in poultry feeds. It also notes anti-nutritional factors that reduce protein quality and nutrient digestibility, including protease inhibitors, non-starch polysaccharides, pectins, and phenolic compounds.

The broader University of Kentucky legume review confirms that cowpeas are black-eyed peas and describes antinutritional factors as a general limit on legume use in poultry feed. It distinguishes field peas, for which low antinutritional factors eliminate the need for heat treatment, from other legumes; it does not establish an equivalent blanket no-processing rule for cowpeas.

## Interim interpretation

The current selector message—“Excluded until feed-grade processing, cultivar, and safe inclusion guidance are confirmed.”—is too generic and does not communicate the existing evidence clearly. The evidence supports a future **conditional** treatment for chickens: cowpeas can be included in a poultry-feed context, but their inclusion rate, product form, and diet balancing must be specified. This evidence does not yet establish a safe raw/inclusion rule for pigeons, parrots, African greys, budgies, or canaries.

## Proposed approval-gated next step

Replace the generic black-eyed-pea message only after the product owner approves species-specific text, for example:

> **Chickens:** Cowpeas (black-eyed peas) are used in poultry feeds, but their anti-nutritional factors can reduce nutrient availability. Use a feed-grade, properly formulated poultry product or seek poultry-nutrition guidance on inclusion rate.

> **Other supported birds:** Black-eyed peas are not offered automatically because the project does not yet hold species-specific preparation and inclusion guidance.

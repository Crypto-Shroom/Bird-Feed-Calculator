# Adzuki Beans: Evidence Review

**Status:** Practical preparation guidance implemented. Adzuki beans are eligible in the calculator with a cooked-or-properly-processed requirement; nutrition values and profile targets are unchanged.

## Current app guidance

The app labels adzuki beans as **cooked or properly processed small red beans**. Its preparation panel instructs users to soak, then boil until completely soft, drain, and cool. The selected-bird note gives whole/split use for pigeons, balanced-ration use for chickens, plain soft cooked portions for parrots/African greys, and finely chopped or mashed portions for budgies/canaries. Soaking and heat preparation are supported by the processing evidence below.

## Existing project record

The ingredient record labels `adzuki_beans` only as “Small red beans.” The historical safe-raw-legume set contains `adzuki_beans`, while the audit processing filter blocks it. Unlike chickpeas, the preserved record does not state a preparation method, inclusion limit, bird species, cultivar, or source.

## Evidence review result

The reviewed poultry-legume overview classifies adzuki bean (*Vigna angularis*) among minor pulses, but does not present it as one of the four grain legumes for which it reviews poultry feeding value, processing, and practical inclusion. [1] A targeted review did not locate a source sufficiently specific to establish an adzuki-bean preparation or inclusion rule for any of the six birds supported by this app.

This is a **source-mapping gap**, not evidence that generic adzuki beans are equivalent to an explicit raw-toxicity ingredient. The legacy “safe raw” membership also has no preserved source note that explains the classification.

## Approval-gated choice

Retain the processing filter for adzuki beans until the project holds an identified product form, credible species-specific source, preparation method, and inclusion policy. If the product owner wishes to show the historical entry in the selector before that review is complete, the message should be amber and factual—for example, “Adzuki bean preparation and bird-specific inclusion guidance are not yet documented in this planner”—rather than a claim of proven toxicity.

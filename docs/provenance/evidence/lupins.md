# Lupins: Evidence Review

**Status:** Evidence recorded; no eligibility, safety rule, nutrient value, profile target, or formula has been changed.

## Existing project record

The generic `lupins` record states “Very high protein, high fiber.” It is currently blocked by the audit processing filter. The record does not identify a *Lupinus* species, whether it is a sweet or bitter cultivar, its alkaloid analysis, product form, or intended bird/inclusion level.

## External evidence

The poultry-legume overview explains that the label “lupin” is not nutritionally or toxicologically specific. It distinguishes high-alkaloid bitter lupins from low-alkaloid sweet lupins and reports that bitter-lupin alkaloids can depress intake, growth, and feed efficiency. It also reports that a sweet-lupin product’s nutrient composition and practical inclusion vary by cultivar and formulation. [1]

> The usable feed category in the reviewed poultry literature is **identified low-alkaloid (“sweet”) lupin**, not an unspecified bag labelled merely “lupins.” [1]

## Interpretation for this app

The generic name is insufficient to make an automatic eligibility decision. The audit filter correctly avoids treating all lupins as interchangeable, but the record should not be portrayed as if every lupin has the same risk profile. There is a clear distinction between an unidentified/bitter product and an identified low-alkaloid sweet feed-grade product.

| Question | Evidence-based status |
|---|---|
| Is generic `lupins` specific enough for automatic formula use? | **No.** |
| Are low-alkaloid sweet lupins documented in poultry diets? | **Yes, conditionally**; practical inclusion still depends on product analysis and ration formulation. [1] |
| Does the source provide a pigeon/parrot-specific automatic rule? | **No.** |
| Is a live rule change justified before collecting cultivar/product evidence? | **No.** |

## Approval-gated choice

Keep generic `lupins` out of automatic formulas. If the product owner wants support for a verified product later, add a **new, distinct record** such as “feed-grade sweet lupin (identified cultivar/analysis)” with a source, product-form rule, and bird-specific policy. Do not reclassify the existing generic `lupins` record as safe or unsafe without that distinction.

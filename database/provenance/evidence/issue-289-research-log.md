# Issue #289 research log: parrot, African Grey and chicken diet ranges

Research done 2026-10-20 to 2026-10-23. Sources are registered in `sources.json`. This log records provenance only; it changes no runtime value (a ledger record does not authorize a calculator change).

## Question
Which protein, fat and fibre ranges, and which diet shares, do authoritative sources support for parrots, African Greys and chickens, given that a grain/seed mix is only part of their diet?

## Sources consulted
Merck Veterinary Manual psittacine page and owner page; Brightsmith 2012; VCA African Grey handout; UC Davis diet-conversion handout; Oregon State PNW 477; Penn State, Kentucky/eXtension and Merck laying-hen pages; Hirano 2010, Harper & Skinner 1998, Péron & Grosset 2013 and Seemanthini & Jani 2020; abstracts of Ullrey 1991, Hess 2002, Hoyle & Garlich 1987, Bavelaar & Beynen 2003 and Voltura 2024; Gilardi & Toft 2012; Luescher (ed.) 2006, chapter 6 and every page on diet, feeding or foraging; Orosz 2014 and Burns 2024. Where only an abstract was read, the source record says so.

## Not consulted or not confirmed
- Koutsos, Matson & Klasing 2001 (Psittaciformes review; registered as `koutsos-2001-psittacines`): full text not consulted for this issue; its chapter-6 figures are known only through Luescher 2006.
- University of Maryland Extension scratch-share figure (about 10%): not confirmed; not used.
- A Spanish clinic quoted in a press article (60% seed): not consulted; contradicts every veterinary source; not used.
- Dutch (Wageningen, CVB tables) and German (LfL Bayern, Landwirtschaftskammer) hobby-chicken guidance: searched, no authoritative source found.
- Retailer and feed-manufacturer pages (Lafeber products, Harrison's, Purina, Versele-Laga, BayWa): not evidence.

Owner decision 2026-10-23: take Brightsmith 2012 as the leading source for the Parrot profile. It covers Amazon parrots only, so for African Grey the species-specific Merck figures take priority and Brightsmith is at most a `related_species` source.

Luescher (ed.) 2006 (checked 2026-10-23) adds no diet split and no fibre, fat, carbohydrate, calcium (parrot), moult or breeding-parrot figure; its only parrot-specific protein figure is the African Grey 10-15%. Energy 154.6-226.1 kcal/kg^0.75 (p. 53) and foraging time (wild Puerto Rican Amazon four to six hours a day; captive Orange-winged Amazons 30-72 minutes on pellets, pp. 197, 304) are recorded for context and support no calculator range. Cockatiel figures (11% adult, 20% chick protein) are not budgie or parrot evidence.

Orosz 2014 and Burns 2024 (checked 2026-10-23) are not independent: Burns cites Orosz and follows its wording, both authors consult for Lafeber Company, and their foraging times come from the same two sources as Luescher 2006. They add: formulated diet as the base; table food no more than 25-35% of the diet for large birds (Orosz, no reference); psittacine total protein minimum 12% (Orosz Table 2, matching the 12% AAFCO figure in Hirano 2010); fat of approximately 4% for granivorous companion birds (Burns, citing MacWhirter 2009 and Brue 1994). They give no total-fat figure for parrots in Orosz, no fibre, no carbohydrate, and no species-specific nutrient figures for African Grey, Amazon, cockatoo or macaw. Fat evidence now spans 4% (Burns, granivores), 4-7% (Brightsmith, Amazons) and 5-12% (Merck, psittacines).

Péron & Grosset 2014 and Harper & Skinner 1998 (extracted 2026-10-23, already registered; their records now carry the details and the journal for Harper & Skinner is corrected to Seminars in Avian and Exotic Pet Medicine). New diet-share support: 80% pellets and 20% fruit and vegetables (Péron & Grosset, citing Reid and Perlberg 1998). Conflict to carry into any decision on breeding and moult protein: Luescher 2006 and Orosz 2014 say needs rise, Harper & Skinner say breeding birds do not appear to need more protein, and Péron & Grosset say moult may not change the diet formulation. None of the four gives a figure for parrots. African Greys held normal uric acid at up to 30% dietary protein (Kamphues et al. 1997, cited by Harper & Skinner), which is a safety observation, not a requirement.

Bavelaar & Beynen 2003 (full text read 2026-10-23; the source id was corrected from 2004 to 2003): the guideline for adult parrot maintenance is expressed per MJ of energy (protein above 9.7 g/MJ, about 12% at 12.4 MJ/kg; calcium 0.2-1.0 g/MJ), so a percentage range only means something together with the mix's energy density. It adds no total-fat or fibre requirement.

Parrot feeding types (researched 2026-10-28). The classification into granivory (budgies, cockatiels), frugivory (many macaws, for example the green-winged macaw) and nectarivory (lorikeets and lories) appears word for word in Orosz (Lafeber, `lafeber-orosz-avian-nutrition-revisited`), shorter in Orosz 2007 and in Reavill 2011, and is attributed to Koutsos, Matson and Klasing 2001 (`koutsos-2001-psittacines`, full text not consulted). Literature labels: budgerigar and cockatiel granivores; hyacinth macaw granivore (palm nuts, about 50% lipid); green-winged macaw and orange-winged Amazon frugivores; scarlet macaw frugivorous-granivorous; sulphur-crested cockatoo omnivore; lories and lorikeets nectarivores. No class in the veterinary sources for lovebird, African Grey, Timneh, conure, Eclectus, Pionus or other Amazons. Conflicts: field studies of wild macaws and Amazons record seeds as the largest plant part (Matsufuji 2021, Gilardi & Toft 2012) even for species labelled frugivores; the wild Congo grey study found fruit most frequent; hyacinth macaw is a "granivore" with a palm-nut, high-fat diet. Maintenance protein by nitrogen balance (Pryor 2001): red lory 1.0%, Pesquet's parrot 3.2%, budgerigar 8.2%; these are minimums from small adult trials. No fibre or carbohydrate requirement for any type; no peer-reviewed wild-diet study for the Timneh grey. Not consulted: Koutsos 2001, Jones 1987 (cockatiel feeding ecology), full texts of Burns 2024 and Orosz 2014.

Gap research (2026-10-28), parrots. No recommended crude fibre or carbohydrate level was found for any parrot after searching reviews, the expert-panel table, Merck, Orosz, McDonald 2006, zoo and AZA material and the Hannover pet-bird symposium abstracts; only measured values exist (Amazon total dietary fibre 6.55% offered and 5.9% consumed, dry weight; hyacinth macaw 3.81% crude fibre in a two-bird maintenance diet) and one trial in which added fibre lowered nutrient digestibility in blue-and-gold macaws. The only numeric breeder figures are McDonald 2006 Table 4.1.33 (dry matter, estimates only, rows unsourced): protein 15-22%, fat 10-15%, calcium 0.7-1.2%, against maintenance protein 10-15% and fat 5%. No moult trial exists; sources conflict on whether moult needs a higher dietary percentage. No requirement study was found for Eclectus, Pionus or hyacinth macaws; no fat or fibre target for cockatiels or lovebirds. AAFCO has no psittacine profile: the "AAFCO 1998" cited in Hirano 2010 is a trade-journal expert-panel report (gross energy 3200-4200 kcal/kg, protein at least 12%, no fat or fibre row) that was never adopted. Not read: Koutsos 2001 review, Drepper 1988, Angel and Ballam 1995, Dierenfeld and Graffam 1996, Valdez 2003, Stanford 2005, full tables of Werquin 2005.

Gap research (2026-10-28), chickens. No NRC, Merck, Hy-Line or Lohmann requirement exists for crude fat or crude fibre in layer, grower or maintenance feed; only linoleic acid (about 1-2.35%). Crude fibre recommendations exist for commercial layers (Lohmann 5-6% pullet feed; LfL 3.5-5.0% in laying phases). No percentage exists for non-laying hens (one per-metabolic-weight estimate, Brainer 2015). No natural-moult protein study exists; a forced-moult trial found 16.5% beat 14.5% (Mendonça and Lima 1999) and feed companies claim 16-20%. No source states carbohydrate; starch plus sugar of grains is 55-65% (CVB). Kentucky regulators give grain scratches 7-10% crude protein. No Dutch or German hobby-flock guidance with numbers was found; the 90/10 rule could not be confirmed from a readable university page (Oregon State says 10-15%, a manufacturer 10%). Not read: University of Maryland, Oklahoma State, Maine and Georgia extension pages, NRC 1994 ingredient table.

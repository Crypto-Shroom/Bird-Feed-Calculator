# Issue #289 research log: parrot, African Grey and chicken diet ranges

Research done 2026-10-20 to 2026-10-23. Sources are registered in `sources.json`. This log records provenance only; it changes no runtime value (a ledger record does not authorize a calculator change).

## Question
Which protein, fat and fibre ranges, and which diet shares, do authoritative sources support for parrots, African Greys and chickens, given that a grain/seed mix is only part of their diet?

## Sources consulted
Merck Veterinary Manual psittacine page and owner page; Brightsmith 2012; VCA African Grey handout; UC Davis diet-conversion handout; Oregon State PNW 477; Penn State, Kentucky/eXtension and Merck laying-hen pages; Hirano 2010, Harper & Skinner 1998, Péron & Grosset 2013 and Seemanthini & Jani 2020; abstracts of Ullrey 1991, Hess 2002, Hoyle & Garlich 1987, Bavelaar & Beynen 2004 and Voltura 2024; Gilardi & Toft 2012; Luescher (ed.) 2006, chapter 6 and every page on diet, feeding or foraging; Orosz 2014 and Burns 2024. Where only an abstract was read, the source record says so.

## Not consulted or not confirmed
- Koutsos, Matson & Klasing 2001 (Psittaciformes review; registered as `koutsos-2001-psittacines`): full text not consulted for this issue; its chapter-6 figures are known only through Luescher 2006.
- University of Maryland Extension scratch-share figure (about 10%): not confirmed; not used.
- Bavelaar & Beynen 2004: abstract only, full text not consulted.
- A Spanish clinic quoted in a press article (60% seed): not consulted; contradicts every veterinary source; not used.
- Dutch (Wageningen, CVB tables) and German (LfL Bayern, Landwirtschaftskammer) hobby-chicken guidance: searched, no authoritative source found.
- Retailer and feed-manufacturer pages (Lafeber products, Harrison's, Purina, Versele-Laga, BayWa): not evidence.

Owner decision 2026-10-23: take Brightsmith 2012 as the leading source for the Parrot profile. It covers Amazon parrots only, so for African Grey the species-specific Merck figures take priority and Brightsmith is at most a `related_species` source.

Luescher (ed.) 2006 (checked 2026-10-23) adds no diet split and no fibre, fat, carbohydrate, calcium (parrot), moult or breeding-parrot figure; its only parrot-specific protein figure is the African Grey 10-15%. Energy 154.6-226.1 kcal/kg^0.75 (p. 53) and foraging time (wild Puerto Rican Amazon four to six hours a day; captive Orange-winged Amazons 30-72 minutes on pellets, pp. 197, 304) are recorded for context and support no calculator range. Cockatiel figures (11% adult, 20% chick protein) are not budgie or parrot evidence.

Orosz 2014 and Burns 2024 (checked 2026-10-23) are not independent: Burns cites Orosz and follows its wording, both authors consult for Lafeber Company, and their foraging times come from the same two sources as Luescher 2006. They add: formulated diet as the base; table food no more than 25-35% of the diet for large birds (Orosz, no reference); psittacine total protein minimum 12% (Orosz Table 2, matching the 12% AAFCO figure in Hirano 2010); fat of approximately 4% for granivorous companion birds (Burns, citing MacWhirter 2009 and Brue 1994). They give no total-fat figure for parrots in Orosz, no fibre, no carbohydrate, and no species-specific nutrient figures for African Grey, Amazon, cockatoo or macaw. Fat evidence now spans 4% (Burns, granivores), 4-7% (Brightsmith, Amazons) and 5-12% (Merck, psittacines).

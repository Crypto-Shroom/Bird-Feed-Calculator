# Issue #171 — Seven dry grain forms: six-bird evidence research log

## Scope and research policy

**AS_OF:** 2026-08-22  
**Mode:** Standard, evidence-ledger research; sequential execution because independent tool parallelism is unavailable in this task.  
**Source policy:** Public sources only. Prefer veterinary references, peer-reviewed primary studies/reviews, extension or academic sources, and documented species-specific husbandry guidance. Community or retail material may identify terminology but does not establish an outcome alone.  
**Counter-review plan:** Test whether any apparent support is actually for a different grain, an adjacent botanical, a processed/cooked/sprouted form, a different bird, or a quantity/complete-ration claim that cannot support ordinary dry grain use.

### Replacement-search checkpoint — pigeon rye/triticale; budgie spelt/buckwheat

A focused replacement search was run for exact pigeon rye/triticale and budgie spelt/buckwheat sources. ### Verified direct sources — pigeon × whole dry rye and triticale

**Triticale:** Savas et al. (2007), a peer-reviewed domestic-pigeon welfare study, states that the pigeons’ study diet contained wheat and triticale grain mixed on a fresh-weight basis. The accessible abstract establishes species and study context; the composition detail is visible in the indexed article record but not its accessible abstract. It supports a **study-bounded direct candidate** for triticale grain in domestic pigeons, subject to a locator that quotes the diet-composition sentence and to an explicit limit: the study investigates beak length/feed intake, not nutritional adequacy, amount, or a formula.

**Rye / ryecorn:** Dilks (1975) fully documents crop-content sampling in feral *Columba livia* across a year. It identifies *Secale cereale* ryecorn in crop contents and reports consumption from newly sown crops. This is direct pigeon consumption evidence for a whole rye-derived grain in a field setting, but it does not prove domestic companion-pigeon nutritional adequacy, a safe quantity, or a ration. It can support only a tightly bounded `limited` candidate if the taxonomy/form mapping is preserved.

**Milo corroboration:** A University of California pigeon nutrition trial used a whole-grain basal mixture containing 30% kafir and milo alongside corn, peas, wheat, grit, and supplements. This independently corroborates the direct pigeon whole-milo context and reinforces the mixed-ration/no-stand-alone boundary.

## Full-source verification — psittacine and budgie grain sources

**Ullrey, Allen & Baer (1991):** The full PubMed abstract states commercial psittacine seed mixtures commonly contain **buckwheat** and that other seeds may be present. It also states that seed mixtures are nutritionally deficient and that selective consumption of seed/pellet mixtures can obstruct correction of those deficiencies. It therefore supports only a group-level, commercial-seed-mixture context for dry buckwheat in psittacines—not a complete ration, a quantity, or a species-specific African-Grey result. The accessible abstract does **not** name milo, rye, spelt, triticale, or either rice form; those remain outside this source’s exact-form support.

**Wyndham (1980):** The full abstract directly studies wild budgerigars and states they ate seeds from ground vegetation, with observed seed lengths about 0.5–2.5 mm. Its full text was not accessible and its abstract does not identify sorghum by name. Search-indexed text had named commercial sorghum, but that uninspected passage is not used as evidence. The source supplies wild-budgie seed ecology only; it does not establish sorghum, a captive diet, or a dry-form outcome.

**VCA African Grey Feeding:** The species-specific exotics-vet page explicitly lists **brown rice** among suggested food items and locates it within a pellet-led diet: pellets approximately 75–80%, fruits/vegetables and small seed amounts otherwise. It also warns that predominantly seed-based diets can cause calcium and other nutrient deficiencies. This directly supports a bounded African-Grey `limited` candidate for plain brown rice, but gives no white-rice, sorghum/milo, rye, triticale, spelt, or buckwheat outcome.

## Full-source verification — poultry extension detail and source-access results

**Rye:** The inspected University of Kentucky Poultry Extension page explicitly identifies *Secale cereale* grain, rejects it for growing chickens/broilers/pullets, allows it for layers only after peak production and below 40% of diet, and identifies both viscous arabinoxylans and potential ergot contamination. This will support a direct chicken `limited` row with explicit growing-bird and clean-grain boundaries.

**Buckwheat:** The same extension programme describes buckwheat broadly as a feed ingredient, specifies protease inhibitors/tannins, notes reduced feed efficiency at high inclusion, and gives a maximum 30% recommendation for outdoor-raised poultry because of fagopyrin/UV sensitivity. It does not identify dehulled groats specifically, so the chicken result must be worded as a buckwheat-seed/groat ingredient boundary rather than a claim that the plant’s aerial material is equivalent.

**Sorghum and rice extension pages:** The anticipated sorghum URL returned a not-found page, and the rice URL did not yield extractable content. Neither is used in the ledger. The search continues to rely on the separately verified direct pigeon whole-milo study and the specific rice studies/source records rather than assuming an extension article exists.

## Full-source verification — whole sorghum and rice-form boundary in chickens

**Fernandes et al. (2013):** A controlled study of 2,400 broilers explicitly compared ground, 50% whole, and 100% whole sorghum grain in nutritionally matched feeds. Pre-starter feeds through day 8 used ground grain; whole sorghum commenced after that. At 21 and 42 days, the tested sorghum physical forms did not reduce performance; the paper concludes whole sorghum is feasible for broilers older than nine days. This is direct chicken evidence for whole dry sorghum within the defined broiler/feed formulation/age boundary only.

**Nanto et al. (2012):** The inspected chicken study concerns **paddy rice**, comparing dehulled, crushed, and untreated whole-grain paddy rice. It does not establish the selected polished **white rice** form. Its contrast of forms confirms white rice, brown rice, and paddy rice must remain separate records. It is excluded from the white-rice outcome rather than being used as a shortcut.

### Final replacement-search result — pigeon × buckwheat groats and spelt

A targeted recovery search for the reported 20%-buckwheat pigeon study and exact whole-spelt evidence returned general seed-preference literature, broad pigeon nutrition reviews, historical agriculture material, and non-exact source references but did not recover an inspectable primary or veterinary source establishing either selected pigeon form. The first-pass buckwheat lead is therefore withdrawn as unverified. Neither form will receive a `limited` row merely because pigeons are granivorous or because another *Columba* species consumes a related food.

### Verified direct source — chicken × plain dry brown rice grain

Nanto-Hara et al. (2021) identifies brown rice as unpolished rice retaining bran and germ and tests it as 57.65% of a corn-replacement, soybean-meal, vitamin-and-mineral complete broiler diet. In Ross 308 broilers from day 12, the study reports performance improvement under both thermoneutral and chronic-heat-stress conditions. Nishikawa et al. (2024) independently tests corn replacement with brown rice in 21- to 42-day broiler diets. Together these are direct chicken evidence for plain dry brown rice as a defined formulated-feed ingredient; they do not approve an amount, complete ration, or another species/form.

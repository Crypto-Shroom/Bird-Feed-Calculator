# Issue #150 research log — five core dry grain forms

## Form boundaries confirmed before research

| Food | Exact review form |
| --- | --- |
| Wheat | Whole dry grain, threshed/hulled where applicable. |
| Barley | Whole dry barley grain, non-pearled; pearled, cooked, sprouted, and processed forms remain distinct. |
| Oats | Whole dry oat grain as the catalog `oats` form; groats, rolled oats, cooked oats, and processed products remain distinct. |
| Millet | Whole dry seed; cultivar/color distinctions remain outside this initial generic record unless a source makes them material. |
| Corn | Whole dry maize/corn kernel, cultivar/color unspecified; fresh, cooked, cracked/ground, popped, and processed forms remain distinct. |

## First-pass research

### Pigeon — whole dry wheat grain

- [Liu et al. (2023)](https://doi.org/10.3390/life13030848) is a direct *Columba livia* production study. Parent pigeons were offered whole corn, whole wheat, whole peas, and pelleted feed separately; the T2 treatment used whole corn, whole wheat, and pelleted feed. The study reports whole grains were accepted and evaluates squab outcomes. It supports a bounded `limited` outcome for whole dry wheat as one component in a defined whole-grain-plus-pellet production feeding system, not a sole diet, pet-ration rule, formula, portion, or nutrition-value claim.
- [Petco veterinary-technician answer (2019; page updated 2025)](https://www.petco.com/pet-education/bird/vet-qa/pigeon-feed-wheat-soaking) states wheat may be fed as a snack and emphasizes a balanced diet. It is direct pigeon context and corroborates the non-staple/balanced-diet boundary, but is lower-tier owner-facing veterinary guidance rather than a controlled feeding trial.

First-pass preliminary outcome: direct pigeon evidence supports only a `limited` whole-dry-wheat context. A targeted follow-up will check for a higher-quality pigeon veterinary or feeding source and any form/whole-grain limitation.


### Parrot — whole dry wheat grain

- [Parrot Awareness Week, Jones BVetMed MRCVS, *Dietary Needs of Parrots*](https://www.parrotawarenessweek.com/diet) describes traditional dry parrot seed mixes as containing millets, oats, wheat, dried corn, safflower, sunflower, and nuts. It warns that a single general dry mix is not a balanced diet for all parrot species. This is direct parrot veterinary/author guidance naming dry wheat in a seed-mix context, but it cannot establish a universal parrot portion, a sole-diet rule, formula inclusion, or another species outcome.
- [Best Friends Animal Society, *The Best Parrot Diet*](https://bestfriends.org/pet-care-resources/best-parrot-diet-and-toxic-foods-avoid) frames cooked whole grains as a fresh-food component and distinguishes them from seed mixes. It does not name whole dry wheat, but corroborates that seed-based feeding should not be treated as a complete diet.

First-pass preliminary outcome: `limited` dry-wheat context for parrots under the named traditional dry-mix source, with an explicit mixed/balanced-diet boundary. A targeted follow-up will seek a higher-quality exact whole-dry-wheat source and test whether a more conservative outcome is required.

### Pigeon — whole dry barley, millet, and corn

- [Hullar et al. (1999), *Studies on the energy content of pigeon feeds I*](https://doi.org/10.1093/ps/78.12.1757) directly studied ten adult male homing pigeons. Corn, wheat, barley, red/white millet and other feeds were each fed **alone, in whole-grain form, ad libitum**, with water and grit; digestibility and metabolizable energy were determined. This is direct evidence that whole dry corn, barley, and millet were used in controlled pigeon feed trials. Its boundary is experimental metabolism/digestibility only: it does not establish a balanced pet ration, portion, sole-diet safety, or a preferred mix.
- [Brown (1969), *Seed Selection by Pigeons*](https://www.jstor.org/stable/4533279), as reported in indexed abstract form, exposed captive pigeons to mixtures containing maize, millet, and a grain component of wheat, oats and barley. It supports consumption/choice context but is not the primary basis for a health outcome because the grain component combined three forms.

First-pass preliminary outcome: `limited` for pigeon whole dry barley, millet, and corn, bounded to controlled whole-grain feed/energy and consumption context. A targeted follow-up will seek a direct pigeon companion-care or production nutrition source before finalization.

### Chicken — whole dry wheat, barley, oats, millet, and corn

- [MOFGA, *Feeding Whole Grains to Chickens*](https://www.mofga.org/resources/fact-sheets/feeding-whole-grains-to-chickens/) provides poultry-extension context for whole grains. It names corn, wheat, barley, oats, and millet; says no grain is ideal alone; identifies formulation limits including 25% starter-diet limits for oats/barley and balanced-ration requirements. It supports only a limited, balanced-ration/age-aware context for all five forms.
- [Wilson (1944), *Corn, Wheat, and Barley for Chickens*](https://openprairie.sdstate.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1375&context=agexperimentsta_bulletins) directly reports chicken experiments: corresponding whole corn, wheat, and barley scratch grain was provided with mash, water, oyster shell, and grit. It reports comparative production, consumption, and formulation results, not a sole-grain home-feeding endorsement.

First-pass preliminary outcome: `limited` for chicken whole dry wheat, barley, oats, millet, and corn only within a formulated/age-aware balanced-ration context. Targeted follow-up will seek direct peer-reviewed support for whole oats and millet, plus a current poultry-veterinary or extension corroboration for the common limitation.


### Parrot — whole dry barley, oats, millet, and corn

- [Omlet, *Seed for Parrots*](https://www.omlet.us/guide/parrots/parrot_food/seed/) names barley, dried corn, red/yellow/white millet, and whole oats as components of homemade parrot seed mixes. It says seeds form 15–50% of a parrot diet depending on breed and says fresh food remains necessary. This supports a bounded `limited` dry-mix context for those named forms, not a complete-diet, portion, or species-specific medical claim.
- The product page returned by the search is not used as scientific evidence. It independently confirms commercial parrot mixes may contain corn, oat groats, millet, and ground grains, but composition does not establish health or suitability.

First-pass preliminary outcome: `limited` for parrot whole dry barley, oats, millet, and corn only as components of a varied seed mix. A targeted follow-up will seek direct avian-veterinary confirmation or retain this bounded care-source conclusion with its limitations.

### Canary — targeted follow-up conclusion

- [Harper and Turner (2000), *Nutrition and energetics of the canary*](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0305-0491(00)00210-8) is a direct peer-reviewed canary nutrition review. It states captive canaries prefer seeds including **millet**, and reports canary energy and dry-matter intake context. It supports a `limited` whole-dry-millet outcome only as part of a varied seed diet; it does not name wheat, barley, oats, or corn.
- [Meander Valley Vets, *Feeding Your Canary*](https://www.meandervets.com.au/blog/feeding-your-canary) is direct canary veterinary guidance that describes varied seasonal seed consumption, warns against selective feeding from narrow seed mixes, and says millet is commonly preferred. It corroborates the varied-diet limitation for millet but does not establish the other exact forms.

Second-pass conclusion: whole dry **millet** is supported as `limited` for canaries within a varied seed/balanced diet. Exact whole dry wheat, non-pearled barley, whole oats, and whole dry corn remain `unresolved` after both passes because the sources did not identify those forms. No cross-species inference applied.

### Pigeon — whole dry oat grain, targeted follow-up conclusion

Second-pass searches used `pigeon whole oats grain feeding study Columba livia`, `pigeon whole oat grain digestibility nutrition`, and `Columba livia oats whole grain diet`. The accessible domestic-pigeon nutrition review confirms adult pigeons are commonly fed mixtures of whole grains but names maize and barley in its accessible abstract, not oats. The accessible Rock Pigeon natural-history landing page did not expose the diet section content; snippets are not treated as evidence. Other results were keeper/social material, retail guidance, or references without accessible exact oat methods/outcomes.

Second-pass conclusion: whole dry oat grain remains `unresolved` for pigeons. It would be improper to infer an oat outcome from wheat/barley/millet/corn trials, general whole-grain guidance, or inaccessible search snippets.

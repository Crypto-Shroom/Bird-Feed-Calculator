**Status:** Complete — canonical provenance research log.

**Scope:** Commercially prepared edible cashew kernel (*Anacardium occidentale*), evaluated for two distinct exact forms:
1. `plain raw cashew kernel, unsalted, unflavoured, and shell-free`
2. `plain dry-roasted cashew kernel, unsalted, unflavoured, and shell-free`

**Non-goals:** Evidence-only research record. It does not add an active ingredient catalog item, nutrient row, optimizer choice, inventory default, safety rule, or visitor-facing wording to `v3-webapp/client/src/lib/data.ts` or `v3-webapp/client/src/lib/safety.ts`.

## P0 — Source Governance & Safety Boundaries

All retained evidence is public. The research distinguishes commercially prepared edible kernels from raw cashew shells, husk liquid, or cashew apples containing cardol and anacardic acid (urushiol-related compounds), which are toxic and excluded.

### Processing & Form Exclusions
- **In scope:** Shell-free, unsalted, unflavoured, plain raw or plain dry-roasted cashew kernels, chopped into manageable or small-seed-sized pieces.
- **Excluded:** Shell-on / unprocessed cashew material, oil-roasted, salted, spiced, seasoned, chocolate- or yogurt-coated cashews, rancid or moldy nuts, whole oversized nuts for small species, and cashew reject meal (logged separately for poultry context).

## P3 — Counter-Review & Verification

1. **Chicken Cashew Reject Meal vs. Whole Kernels:** Akande et al. evaluated processed full-fat and defatted cashew reject meal in balanced layer rations. This cannot be transferred to whole or chopped cashew kernels fed to backyard chickens or companion birds.
2. **Raw vs. Dry-Roasted Distinction:** Raw and dry-roasted cashews are distinct food forms. Parrot guidance explicitly accepts both forms in moderation, while pigeon guidance establishes preparation limits (small seed size) for raw cashew, leaving dry-roasted unresolved.
3. **High Fat Density Boundary:** Cashews contain ~47–50% fat. For all species where limited use is established (pigeons, parrots), strict portion caps (5–10% of total diet) are required to prevent hepatic lipidosis, obesity, and crop slow-down.
4. **No Active Code / Data Alteration:** This research log provides provenance traceability. No active ingredient catalog (`data.ts`), safety rules (`safety.ts`), or target profiles (`birds.ts`) are changed.

## P0 — Source Governance & Safety Boundaries

### User Request & Source Context
The research request arises from owner reports regarding pine nuts (also referred to interchangeably as pine seeds, piñon nuts, or cedar nuts in aviculture and commercial seed mixes). A key community warning on The Parrot Club forum ([`theparrotclub-pinenut-warning-2015`](https://theparrotclub.co.uk/community/index.php?threads/word-of-warning-on-pinenuts.15095/)) highlights two major hazards associated with whole in-shell pine nuts:
1. **Mechanical Beak Entrapment / Wedging Hazard:** In-shell pine nuts can become wedged or stuck tightly onto the lower mandible of medium-to-large psittacines, creating an acute feeding blockage and requiring manual or veterinary intervention with tools (pliers/forceps) to free the bird.
2. **Aspergillus / Fungal Contamination & Mechanical Ingestive Hazard:** In-shell nuts (like peanuts and in-shell pine nuts) stored improperly carry mold/fungal spore risks, while hard shell fragments pose choking or crop obstruction risks for birds incapable of cracking them cleanly.

### Physical Form Boundaries
- **In-shell Pine Nuts (`whole in-shell pine nuts, raw or dry, plain and unseasoned`):** Hard outer shell intact. Unsuitable for small-beaked birds (budgie, canary, pigeon) that cannot crack the hard shell. Medium/large parrots may crack them, but carry a documented risk of lower-mandible shell entrapment and fungal contamination if improperly stored.
- **Shelled Pine Nut Kernels (`plain shelled pine nut kernel, raw or dry-roasted, unsalted and unflavoured`):** Shell-free kernel. High-fat nut meat. Requires chopping to small-seed size for pigeons (who swallow seeds whole without mastication) to prevent crop/esophageal obstruction. Offered strictly in small quantities as a treat for companion birds.
- **Excluded Forms:** Salted, seasoned, roasted in oil, sugared, chocolate-coated, moldy, rancid, or pine cone ornament material.

## P3 — Counter-Review & Reconciliation

1. **In-shell vs. Shelled Kernels:** In-shell pine nuts and shelled pine nut kernels are distinct physical forms with different safety and handling profiles. Small birds (budgies, canaries) and pigeons cannot manage or crack in-shell pine nuts safely, resulting in an `avoid` classification for the in-shell form due to mechanical wedging, choking, and crop obstruction risks.
2. **Shelled Kernels for Pigeons:** Pigeons swallow food whole. Shelled pine nut kernels must be chopped to small-seed size to prevent crop/esophageal obstruction, and offered only as a limited high-fat treat.
3. **Parrot Shell Entrapment Warning:** Parrots eating in-shell pine nuts face a documented risk of the shell wedging over the lower mandible. Keepers must inspect and supervise when offering in-shell nuts as foraging enrichment.
